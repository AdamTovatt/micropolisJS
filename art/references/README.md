# References

Concept sheets of 3×3 zones, seen from above in the game's view, that the zone scenes are modelled on. They are references to look at, never used in a render.

- `residential-sheet.png`: 17 residential zones in 3 rows of 6; the last place in the third row is empty.
- `commercial-sheet.png`: 21 commercial zones in 3 rows of 7.
- `industrial-sheet.png`: 9 industrial zones in 3 rows of 3. Generated by `gemini-3-pro-image` through `tools/generate.py`, with `residential-sheet.png` as its reference image, from:

  > A concept sheet for a city-building game, drawn in exactly the style, view and scale of the reference image: nine square industrial zones, each three by three map tiles, in a grid of 3 rows and 3 columns on a dark grey background, each zone outlined in bright green. Seen from directly above, with the south and west walls just visible, as in the reference. Top left: an empty industrial zone, bare packed earth and gravel with a few weeds and a large pale letter I marked on the ground. The other eight, from small and run-down to large and dense: a small workshop with a yard of crates and a lorry; a scrapyard with heaps of scrap metal and a crane; a warehouse with loading bays and parked lorries; a brick factory with one tall chimney; a chemical works with round storage tanks and pipework; a steel mill with a long shed and a conveyor; a large factory with several chimneys; a dense plant with sawtooth roofs and silos. A green letter I painted on one roof in each zone. No streets crossing the zones, no text labels, no smoke.

`tools/reference.py` crops one zone by row and column, counted from 1 at the top left, or writes a whole sheet with each zone's row and column printed on it:

```bash
python art/tools/reference.py commercial 2 3 tower.png
python art/tools/reference.py residential all overview.png
```

Zone (1, 1) of each sheet is the empty zone: its letter on bare ground.

## Industrial slots

The game picks a populated industrial zone's tiles by land value and density (`placeIndustrial` in `src/industrial.js`): slot = value × 4 + density, nine ids each from 621. Each slot has one design, chosen by how dense and how well-off it looks. A powered zone animates one tile of its slot (`setAnimation`), and `src/animationManager.ts` lists the frames of a second tile in four of the slots too, so a design puts a smoking stack in each tile its slot lists, and renders its frames into `frame-<n>` beside the still zone (`render_animated()`).

| 1 | 630–638 | `industrial_scrapyard` | none |
